The question

More trainers showed up for Community Day. So why did it make less money?

A live-ops team sees this the Monday after an event and has to explain it before the next one. The answer is spread across systems that do not talk to each other: the game server, the store purchase log, and the live-ops calendar. LiveOps Investigator queries all three through Zetaris, follows the evidence step by step, and lets you challenge any step.

  1. MeasureEvery number comes from SQL that Zetaris runs across the three sources.
  2. Follow the evidenceEach result opens the next checks. H-MEM recalls earlier lessons; Nightshift's local model picks among the open checks.
  3. Size each explanationPayment failures, a changed offer, a shift in the player mix: each gets a verdict, a dollar estimate, and the steps behind it. What the checks cannot explain is said out loud.
  4. ChallengeDispute a value, ask for another comparison, or reject a finding. The agent answers with new queries.

What this does not claim

The data is synthetic: generated players, orders and payment attempts loaded into Postgres and queried through Zetaris, not the game operator's or any real game's figures. Revenue is at list price in US dollars, before taxes and store fees. Dollar amounts per explanation are estimates that can overlap. Findings say where to look next; they do not prove a cause. Nightshift only chooses the order of checks the rules have opened, and every open check runs; it never writes SQL or states a number.
